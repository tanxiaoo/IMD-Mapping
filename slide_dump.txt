===== SLIDE 5 =====
Study Area
---
Milan, Italy and Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam ~8,900 km apart, test whether one impervious-mapping method generalises across climate, data and urban form.
---
Milan, Italy — 3M metro residents, temperate climate; impervious cover drives summer heat island intensity and flood risk. The only city with a CLMS reference, alongside GHS-BUILT-S.
---
Hanoi, Vietnam — 8M+ residents on the Red River delta, monsoon climate; impervious expansion directly raises flash-flood risk. No fine-grained product existed before this study beyond GHS-BUILT-S.
---
Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam — 9M+ residents, low-lying delta terrain, among the most flood-exposed cities in Southeast Asia. Tests whether a model trained elsewhere transfers with zero local training data.
---
Supports the Italy-Vietnam LCZ-UHI-GEO project; three climates test whether one method generalises across cities.

===== SLIDE 7 =====
Five data sets, three roles
---
Note: AlphaEarth and Sentinel-2 are the predictors throughout. Sentinel-2 composites use 10 spectral bands: 4 native at 10 m (B2, B3, B4, B8) and 6 native at 20 m (B5, B6, B7, B8A, B11, B12), all resampled to 10 m. CLMS and GHS-BUILT-S are training targets and, scored independently, maps under test. EarthLabel is never used in training.
---
Data set | Type | Resolution | Coverage | Role
AlphaEarth embeddings | 64-band annual satellite embedding | 10 m | Milan, Hanoi, HCMC | Predictor
Sentinel-2 composites | Median / stack / percentile reflectance | 10 m output (10 and 20 m bands) | Milan 30 dates, Hanoi 4, HCMC 3 | Predictor
CLMS Imperviousness Density | Sealed surface, 2018 | 10 m | Milan only (Europe) | Training target, Milan
GHS-BUILT-S | Built-up surface, 2018 | 10 m | Milan, Hanoi, HCMC (global) | Training target, all cities
EarthLabel plots | Photo-interpreted, 2018 imagery | 10 m per plot | 450 plots per city, 1,350 total | Independent validation only

===== SLIDE 10 =====
Stratified spatial sampling and the 1 km-block split
---
• 500 points per IMD class (3,500 total) sampled across Milan, then assigned by whole 1 km block to train or test so nearby points never leak across the split.
• A 250 m buffer removes any block edge-adjacent to the other set, leaving 2,449 train / 1,014 test points, balanced across all seven classes.
---
===== SLIDE 11 =====
Training the random forest, and what it learned
---
• The low percentiles of red (B4) and high percentiles of near-infrared (B8) dominate feature importance — exactly the seasonal extremes a single-date or median composite discards.
• On the 1,014-point spatial holdout, the trained model reaches RMSE 10.86, R² 0.90 — accuracy the raster prediction step then carries onto the full scene.

===== SLIDE 16 =====
Why we check accuracy three different ways
---
This map will get used in different ways once it leaves this study. Some people will read the raw percentage. Some will just want a yes-or-no “is this sealed?” answer for flood and heat planning. And some only care roughly how sealed a place is, not the exact number. A model can look great on one of these and fall short on the other two. That is why we check every map all three ways, against the same 450 EarthLabel plots in each city.
---
A. Continuous error
---
This asks the simplest question: how close are the map's numbers to reality, on average? We look at RMSE, MAE and bias, in IMD percentage points. IMD itself is reported as a percentage, so this is the most direct read of accuracy. It is also the only one of the three that says whether a map runs high or low.

---
B. Binary agreement
---
This asks a much simpler yes-or-no question: is this patch of ground sealed? We check overall accuracy and Cohen's kappa at the 50% cut. Flood and heat-island screening often only needs that yes/no call, and kappa makes sure we're not just getting lucky.

---
C. Ordinal agreement
---
This asks whether the map is roughly right, even when it isn't exact. A map can miss by one class everywhere, or get most places right and blow up badly on a few. Overall accuracy and quadratic weighted kappa (QWK) across the full 10-class scale is what tells those two very different problems apart.

RMSE and MAE average the error, in IMD percentage points, between predicted and observed; RMSE penalises large misses more. Bias is observed minus predicted, so positive means the map under-predicts. OA is the plain share of the 450 plots the map gets right; kappa corrects that for chance agreement at the 50% cut. QWK extends that logic to the full 10-class scale, penalising a far-off miss more than an adjacent-class one.
---

===== SLIDE 21 =====
What this study concludes
---
AlphaEarth and Sentinel-2 map impervious surface almost equally well, as long as both are trained locally.
Zero-shot transfer fails outright. A model really only works well in the city it was trained in.
Local retraining fixes most of that failure, though not all of it.
Only an independent reference tells you real accuracy. Every training product, checked that way, is beaten by the model fitted to it.

===== SLIDE 22 =====
Key findings
---
What we found

Sentinel-2 beats AlphaEarth in Milan, by a clear margin, against CLMS.
The training label matters as much as the features — swapping to GHS-BUILT-S costs about 4 RMSE.
Zero-shot transfer to Vietnam fails outright — every difference map turns solid red.
Local retraining fixes 38 to 67% of GHS-BUILT-S's own under-prediction.
---
What it means

No training label is a ceiling — every model beats the label it learned from.
RMSE, kappa and QWK all agree on the winner, in every city.
HCMC's zero-shot “win” is a coincidence of levels, not real transfer.
Next: a better Vietnamese reference map with roads, and a deeper 2018 Sentinel-2 archive.
