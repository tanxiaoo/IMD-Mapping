# Missing figures

Findings established in `data/FACTS.md` and `data/EXPERIMENT_MAP.md` that no
existing figure carries. Ranked by how central the finding is to the project's
argument.

Last updated: 2026-08-30 · none of these have been built

See `data/FIGURES.md` for what already exists and for the house style any new
figure should match (accent `#0B6E4F`, DejaVu Sans, figsize 11.5–13.0 ×
3.5–4.6 in, dpi 200).

## Ranked

### 1 — Track A vs Track B, paired

**The single most important missing figure.** The four Milan predictor sets on
two axes: agreement with CLMS (Track A holdout) against accuracy versus
photo-interpretation (Track B). A slope chart or connected dumbbell, one line per
predictor set.

**Shows:** a Track A spread of **9.46 → 14.12 RMSE** collapsing to **24.64 →
25.98** against independent validation. Most of the apparent Track A separation
was agreement with CLMS, not accuracy. Percentile leads Track A by 4.7 RMSE but
leads Track B by 1.2.

**Carries:** findings 1 and 2 together — that pairing is the point, and it is why
this ranks first.

**Data:** `data/FACTS.md` Table A (Milan `GEE_RF` rows) and Table B (Milan
`role='model'`, primary rule). Both are already collected by
`code/collect_metrics.py`; no notebook re-run needed.

### 2 — Milan two-tier structure

Pairwise matrix or dumbbell of the six Milan comparisons, with the two
non-significant pairs greyed or dashed.

**Shows:** `S2_stack` and `S2_percentile` are each distinguishable from `emb_RF`
and `S2_median`, but **not from each other** (q = 0.79); `emb_RF` and
`S2_median` are likewise indistinguishable (q = 0.73). Two tiers, not a ranking
of four.

**Carries:** finding 3. Refines the "all four indistinguishable" reading without
contradicting it — paired testing removes plot-level variance and so has more
power than the overlapping RMSE CIs.

**Data:** `outputs_validation/table2_wilcoxon_pairs.csv` (Milan rows), or the
paired-test section of `data/FACTS.md`.

### 3 — Vietnam level-matching

Mean predicted IMD against mean reference IMD, per city × scenario, with the
reference level and GHSL's level as horizontal rules.

**Shows:** both zero-shots inherit Milan's high level (Hanoi ~61.8, HCMC ~57.9
and 70.7); both local retrains inherit GHSL's deficit (~38–40). Hanoi's reference
is 46.05 so the retrains win; HCMC's is 51.06 so the S2 zero-shot wins. **Which
scenario wins is set by the city's reference level, not by transfer quality.**

**Carries:** finding 4 — the result most likely to be misread as "S2-median
transfers well to HCMC".

**Data:** `outputs_validation/validation_per_plot_long.csv`, primary rule,
grouped by (city, map_id); the HCMC figures are already tabulated in
`data/FACTS.md`.

### 4 — Bias recovery against the training target

Arrows or a slope chart from GHSL's bias to each local retrain's bias, per city.

**Shows:** GHSL under-marks by **+19.68 pp** (Hanoi) and **+19.80 pp** (HCMC);
the local retrains are biased only **+6.59 to +12.23**, recovering **7.57–13.09
pp** — 38–67 % of the reference's systematic deficit, more in Hanoi than HCMC.

**Carries:** finding 6 — direct evidence that a model recovers signal its labels
lack, and therefore that the training target is not a ceiling.

**Data:** the bias-recovery table in `data/FACTS.md`, generated by
`collect_bias_recovery()` in `code/collect_metrics.py`.

### 5 — Composite depth

The 2018 calendar with each city's usable acquisition dates marked: Milan 30,
Hanoi 4, HCMC 3 (of which two are five days apart, so ~2 effective looks).

**Shows:** Milan's near-annual composite against Vietnam's dry-season fragments —
and that Milan is *not* annual either, since January, February and May are
absent.

**Carries:** finding 7. Necessary caveat for every Vietnam zero-shot comparison:
the embeddings zero-shot compares annual against annual while the S2 zero-shot
does not, so composite depth is confounded with predictor type.

**Data:** `samples_S2_median/s2_extraction_metadata.json` (Milan, 30 dates) and
`samples_S2_median_{Hanoi,HCMC}/s2_extraction_metadata.json` (`selected_dates`,
`obs_per_pixel`). The composite-date table in `EXPERIMENT_MAP.md` has all of it.

### 6 — CV versus holdout reversal

RF and SVR on both axes: tuning CV RMSE against holdout RMSE, for the embeddings
run.

**Shows:** SVR wins CV at every block size (11.71 vs 13.16 at best block) then
**loses the holdout on all three metrics** (14.76 vs 14.12 RMSE). Should carry
the caveat that holdout rows are GEE-built rasters while CV is sklearn, so the
reversal is confounded with estimator-translation fidelity.

**Carries:** finding 9, and explains why `outputs_v2` metadata records
`best_model_name='SVR'` while RF is the model carried forward.

**Data:** `outputs_v2/hyperparameter_tuning.csv`,
`outputs_v2/spatial_cv_summary.csv`, `outputs_v2/holdout_test_metrics.csv`.

### 7 — Rule sensitivity slopes

Five Milan maps across strict → B → C, one line each.

**Shows:** all five including CLMS improve under rule C (−2.18 to −3.67 RMSE);
ranking is unchanged under B; `emb_RF` and `S2_median` cross between 3rd and 4th
under C — a swap between two maps the paired test finds indistinguishable, so a
reordering within noise.

**Carries:** finding 8.

**Data:** `outputs_validation/table3_rule_sensitivity.csv` (`rank_changed`
already flags the crossing), or the Milan rule-ranking table in `FACTS.md`.

## Not buildable from this repo

**Finding 10 — reproducibility against the earlier AlphaEarth study.** The claim
is that the embeddings baseline (RMSE 14.123 / MAE 10.675 / R² 0.837) matches an
earlier study exactly. **No such reference values exist anywhere in this repo** —
not in `README.md`, not in `docs/` (which holds only an unrelated signed LCZ-UHI
report). The comparison cannot be figured, or verified, until the source is
supplied. Treat the claim as unverified rather than established.
